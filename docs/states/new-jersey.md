# New Jersey DPC Guide

> **Quick Summary:** New Jersey does not have explicit DPC-enabling legislation and has restrictive medication dispensing rules. Dense population but complex regulatory environment. Legal consultation essential.

---

!!! warning "Caution"
    **Verify Current Law:** New Jersey lacks explicit DPC legislation and has restrictive dispensing rules. Consult the [New Jersey State Board of Medical Examiners](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/bme/) and a New Jersey healthcare attorney before proceeding.

## DPC Law Status

### Legislation

**Status:** No explicit DPC-enabling legislation

New Jersey has not enacted specific legislation exempting DPC agreements from insurance regulation.

### What This Means

- Greater legal uncertainty for DPC model
- New Jersey healthcare attorney consultation essential
- Careful agreement structuring critical
- Monitor for legislative developments

---

## Medication Dispensing

### Regulatory Status

New Jersey has **significant restrictions** on physician dispensing.

### Requirements

- **DEA Registration:** Required for controlled substances
- **State Restrictions:** Very limited dispensing circumstances
- **Pharmacy Board:** [njconsumeraffairs.gov/phar](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/phar/)
- **PMP:** Check NJ Prescription Monitoring Program
- **Review Regulations:** Dispensing generally not practical

### Important Note

New Jersey is among the most restrictive states for physician dispensing. Most DPC practices in New Jersey do not dispense medications.

## State-Specific Considerations

### Dense Population

- Most densely populated state
- Large potential patient base
- NYC and Philadelphia suburbs

### High Cost of Living

- Among highest cost-of-living states
- Higher membership fees necessary
- Significant overhead costs

### Commuter Population

- Many residents work in NYC or Philadelphia
- Consider commuter schedules
- After-hours access important

### Complex Regulatory Environment

- More compliance requirements
- Attorney consultation essential

---

## Checklist for Starting DPC in New Jersey

- [ ] **Consult New Jersey healthcare attorney** (essential)
- [ ] Obtain or verify New Jersey medical license
- [ ] Register business entity with Division of Revenue
- [ ] Obtain EIN from IRS
- [ ] Register with DEA (if prescribing controlled substances)
- [ ] **Do not plan to dispense** (restrictions prohibitive)
- [ ] Secure malpractice insurance
- [ ] Develop membership agreement (attorney drafting recommended)
- [ ] File Medicare opt-out affidavit (if applicable)
