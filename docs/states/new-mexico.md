# New Mexico DPC Guide

> **Quick Summary:** New Mexico has DPC-enabling legislation (NMSA § 59A-23E) and permits medication dispensing. Rural character creates primary care opportunities. Cultural diversity and unique healthcare needs.

## DPC Law Status

### Legislation

**Statute:** New Mexico Statutes Annotated § 59A-23E

**Statute URL:** [NMSA Chapter 59A](https://nmonesource.com/nmos/nmsa/en/nav_date.do)

New Mexico has DPC-enabling legislation exempting DPC agreements from insurance regulation.

**Key provisions:**
- DPC agreements not considered insurance
- Primary care services defined
- Written agreement required

### What This Means

- DPC practices have legal clarity
- Standard business regulations apply
- Supportive regulatory environment

---

## Medication Dispensing

### Regulatory Status

New Mexico permits physician dispensing **with registration**.

### Requirements

- **DEA Registration:** Required for controlled substances
- **State Registration:** Register with New Mexico Board of Pharmacy
- **Pharmacy Board:** [rld.state.nm.us/boards/Pharmacy.aspx](https://www.rld.state.nm.us/boards/Pharmacy.aspx)
- **PMP:** Check New Mexico Prescription Monitoring Program
- **Record Keeping:** Maintain dispensing records

## State-Specific Considerations

### Rural and Frontier Areas

- Vast rural areas
- Significant primary care shortage
- Telehealth essential

### Albuquerque and Santa Fe

- Largest population centers
- More competition in metros
- Cultural/arts communities

### Cultural Diversity

- Hispanic and Native American communities
- Consider cultural competency
- Bilingual services valuable

### Indian Health Service

- Federal facilities serve tribal populations
- Coordination considerations

---

## Checklist for Starting DPC in New Mexico

- [ ] Obtain or verify New Mexico medical license
- [ ] Register business entity with Secretary of State
- [ ] Obtain EIN from IRS
- [ ] Register with DEA (if prescribing controlled substances)
- [ ] Register with Board of Pharmacy (if dispensing)
- [ ] Secure malpractice insurance
- [ ] Develop membership agreement
- [ ] Consider bilingual services
- [ ] File Medicare opt-out affidavit (if applicable)
