# California DPC Guide

> **Quick Summary:** California does not have explicit DPC-enabling legislation and has complex healthcare regulations. Medication dispensing has restrictions. Legal consultation strongly recommended before starting DPC in California.

## DPC Law Status

### Legislation

**Status:** No explicit DPC-enabling legislation

California has not enacted specific legislation exempting DPC agreements from insurance regulation. The state's Department of Insurance and Department of Managed Health Care have broad authority.

### What This Means

- Greater legal uncertainty for DPC model
- California healthcare attorney consultation essential
- Careful agreement structuring critical
- More regulatory scrutiny possible

---

## Medication Dispensing

### Regulatory Status

California has **significant restrictions** on physician dispensing.

### Requirements

- **DEA Registration:** Required for controlled substances
- **State Registration:** Register with California Board of Pharmacy
- **Pharmacy Board:** [pharmacy.ca.gov](https://www.pharmacy.ca.gov/)
- **Restrictions:** Dispensing to own patients only, various limitations
- **CURES:** Must check California's PDMP (CURES 2.0)
- **Record Keeping:** Detailed requirements

### Important Limitations

California has more restrictive dispensing rules than many states. Review Board of Pharmacy regulations carefully before implementing dispensing.

## State-Specific Considerations

### Complex Regulatory Environment

- Multiple regulatory agencies
- Attorney consultation essential
- More compliance requirements

### High Cost of Living

- Among highest cost-of-living states
- Higher membership fees may be necessary
- Significant overhead costs

### Large Market

- Most populous state
- Significant opportunity despite challenges
- Growing interest in DPC model

### Established DPC Presence

- Despite challenges, DPC practices exist
- Connect with California DPC physicians for guidance
- Learn from those who've navigated the system

## Checklist for Starting DPC in California

- [ ] **Consult California healthcare attorney** (essential)
- [ ] Understand regulatory landscape before proceeding
- [ ] Obtain or verify California medical license
- [ ] Register professional corporation with Secretary of State
- [ ] Obtain EIN from IRS
- [ ] Register with DEA (if prescribing controlled substances)
- [ ] Understand dispensing restrictions (consult Board of Pharmacy)
- [ ] Secure malpractice insurance (California rates higher)
- [ ] Develop membership agreement (attorney drafting recommended)
- [ ] File Medicare opt-out affidavit (if applicable)
