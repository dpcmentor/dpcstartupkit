# Membership Models Comparison

## Overview

Direct Primary Care practices use membership-based pricing rather than fee-for-service billing. The membership model you choose affects patient perception, administrative complexity, and revenue predictability.

This guide compares common membership structures to help you select the right approach for your practice.

## Prerequisites

- Understanding of your target patient demographics
- Awareness of your local market and competition
- Completed [Startup Costs Overview](../business-formation/startup-costs-overview.md)

---

## Core Membership Models

### Model 1: Flat Monthly Fee (Per Person)

**Structure:** Each patient pays the same monthly fee regardless of age or usage.

**Example Pricing:**
- Adult: $99/month
- Child (under 18): $49/month

**Advantages:**
- Simplest to understand and explain
- Easiest to administer
- Predictable revenue per patient
- No perception of "paying for what you don't use"

**Disadvantages:**
- May undervalue services for high-utilizers
- May feel expensive for low-utilizers
- Doesn't account for different care needs by age

**Best for:** Practices targeting a specific demographic (e.g., young professionals, retirees) with relatively homogeneous care needs.

---

### Model 2: Age-Based Tiered Pricing

**Structure:** Monthly fees vary by age bracket, reflecting different expected care needs.

**Example Pricing:**
| Age Group | Monthly Fee |
|-----------|-------------|
| 0-19 | $50/month |
| 20-44 | $79/month |
| 45-64 | $99/month |
| 65+ | $125/month |

**Advantages:**
- Reflects actual care utilization patterns
- More accessible for younger patients
- Captures more value from higher-need populations
- Perceived as fair by most patients

**Disadvantages:**
- More complex pricing to explain
- Requires age verification
- Patients "age into" higher tiers (communicate clearly)

**Best for:** General family practices serving all ages.

---

### Model 3: Family/Household Pricing

**Structure:** Discounted rates when multiple family members enroll together.

**Example Structures:**

**Option A: Per-Person with Family Cap**
- First adult: $99/month
- Second adult: $89/month
- Each child: $39/month
- Family maximum: $250/month

**Option B: Household Flat Rate**
- Individual: $99/month
- Couple: $175/month
- Family (any size): $225/month

**Option C: Add-On Pricing**
- Primary member: $99/month
- Each additional family member: +$50/month

**Advantages:**
- Encourages whole-family enrollment
- Increases revenue per household
- Simplifies billing for families
- Competitive advantage over insurance

**Disadvantages:**
- Revenue per person decreases with family size
- More complex administration
- Must define "family" carefully (household vs. dependents)

**Best for:** Family medicine practices, pediatric-friendly practices, practices in family-oriented communities.

---

### Model 4: Tiered Service Levels

**Structure:** Multiple membership tiers with different service inclusions.

**Example:**

| Tier | Monthly Fee | Includes |
|------|-------------|----------|
| Basic | $59/month | Office visits, messaging, phone consultations |
| Standard | $99/month | Basic + annual physical, basic labs, telehealth |
| Premium | $149/month | Standard + home visits, extended hours, expedited referrals |

**Advantages:**
- Captures different willingness-to-pay
- Allows patients to choose their level
- Higher revenue potential from premium tier
- Entry-level option for price-sensitive patients

**Disadvantages:**
- Most complex to administer
- Risk of patients feeling "nickel and dimed"
- Must clearly define tier boundaries
- May create perception of two-class care

**Best for:** Markets with diverse economic demographics, practices wanting to maximize revenue while maintaining accessibility.

**Caution:** Many DPC advocates discourage tiered models as they can undermine the DPC philosophy of comprehensive, accessible care.

---

### Model 5: Annual Membership with Monthly Option

**Structure:** Offer both annual (discounted) and monthly payment options.

**Example:**
- Monthly: $99/month ($1,188/year)
- Annual: $999/year (save $189, ~16% discount)

**Advantages:**
- Annual payments improve cash flow
- Committed patients for full year
- Discount rewards commitment
- Reduces payment processing fees

**Disadvantages:**
- Larger upfront cost may deter some patients
- Refund policy needed for early termination
- Must track different payment schedules

**Best for:** Practices with established patient trust, patients with HSA/FSA funds to spend.

## Hybrid and Specialized Models

### Employer/Group Model

**Structure:** Contract with employers to provide DPC for employees.

**Typical Pricing:**
- Per Employee Per Month (PEPM): $50-$150
- Often lower than individual rates due to volume
- May include dependents at additional cost

**Considerations:**
- Requires B2B sales capability
- Contract negotiations more complex
- Payroll deduction simplifies collection
- Volume can accelerate practice growth

---

### Concierge Hybrid

**Structure:** Membership fee plus fee-for-service billing to insurance.

**Note:** This is technically "concierge medicine" rather than pure DPC. The membership provides enhanced access while insurance handles clinical services.

**Considerations:**
- Higher total revenue potential
- Insurance billing complexity remains
- Not "pure" DPC model
- May attract different patient demographic

---

### Retainer + Procedure Fees

**Structure:** Base membership covers routine care; separate fees for procedures, labs, or medications.

**Example:**
- Membership: $79/month (visits, messaging, phone)
- Labs: At cost + 20%
- Procedures: $25-$200 depending on complexity
- Dispensed medications: At cost + 20%

**Advantages:**
- Lower base price attracts price-sensitive patients
- Additional revenue from procedures/labs
- Transparent pricing for each service
- Patients only pay for what they use

**Disadvantages:**
- More complex billing
- Patients may delay needed care to avoid costs
- Less predictable revenue
- May feel less "unlimited"

## Pricing Considerations

### What Should Be Included in Membership?

**Typically Included:**
- Office visits (unlimited or generous limit)
- Secure messaging with physician
- Phone consultations
- Same-day or next-day appointments
- Annual wellness exam
- Basic in-office procedures (ear lavage, wound care, etc.)
- Care coordination
- Prescription management

**Often Included:**
- Telehealth/video visits
- Basic point-of-care testing (strep, flu, UA)
- EKG
- Some basic labs (CBC, metabolic panel, lipids)

**Usually Additional Cost:**
- Wholesale labs (passed through at cost or small markup)
- Imaging
- Procedures requiring supplies (joint injections, biopsies, etc.)
- Dispensed medications
- Vaccines (may include administration, patient pays for vaccine)

**Rarely Included:**
- Specialist referrals (though coordination is included)
- Hospital care
- Emergency services
- Medications (beyond dispensed options)

---

### Pricing Your Membership

See [Pricing Your Practice](pricing-your-practice.md) for detailed guidance. Key factors:

1. **Your costs:** What do you need to cover expenses and earn reasonable income?
2. **Market rates:** What are competitors (DPC and traditional) charging?
3. **Value delivered:** What is comprehensive primary care worth?
4. **Target panel size:** More patients at lower price vs. fewer at higher price?
5. **Local economics:** What can your target patients afford?

**National DPC Pricing Ranges (2024):**
- Low: $50-$70/month (often higher volume models)
- Average: $75-$100/month
- High: $125-$200/month (often lower panel sizes, more comprehensive)

## Model Selection Framework

### Answer These Questions:

**1. Who is your target patient?**
- Young professionals → Simple flat rate may work
- Families with children → Family pricing important
- Mixed demographics → Age-tiered pricing

**2. What is your local economic environment?**
- Affluent area → Higher single price or premium tier
- Mixed income → Consider tiered or lower base + fees
- Price-sensitive → Lean model with lower membership

**3. How do you want to practice?**
- High touch, small panel → Higher prices, simpler structure
- Volume practice → Lower prices, efficient model
- Procedure-focused → Base + procedure fee model

**4. What is your competition?**
- No other DPC → More pricing flexibility
- Established DPC → Differentiate by value or price
- Strong insurance coverage → May need lower prices to compete

**5. What is your administrative capacity?**
- Solo, minimal admin → Simpler model (flat or age-tiered)
- Have admin support → Can handle complex models

## Recommended Starting Models

### For Most New DPC Practices

**Age-Tiered with Family Option:**

| Category | Monthly Fee |
|----------|-------------|
| Children (0-17) | $49 |
| Adults (18-64) | $89 |
| Seniors (65+) | $109 |
| Family Cap | $249/month |

*Annual payment: 10% discount*

**Why This Works:**
- Accessible to most demographics
- Reflects care utilization patterns
- Family pricing encourages household enrollment
- Simple enough to explain and administer

---

### For Premium/Concierge-Style DPC

**Single Comprehensive Rate:**

| Category | Monthly Fee |
|----------|-------------|
| Individual | $175 |
| Couple | $300 |
| Family | $400 |

*Includes: All visits, comprehensive labs, procedures, dispensed generics*

**Why This Works:**
- Premium experience expectation
- Truly "all-inclusive" is easy to understand
- Attracts patients willing to pay for value
- Lower panel size, higher touch

---

### For High-Volume/Accessible DPC

**Low Base with Transparent Add-Ons:**

| Category | Monthly Fee |
|----------|-------------|
| Individual | $59 |
| Each additional family member | +$39 |

*Labs, medications at wholesale cost. Procedures $20-$100.*

**Why This Works:**
- Low barrier to entry
- Transparent pricing builds trust
- Revenue supplemented by add-ons
- Accessible to working-class patients

## Checklist: Selecting Your Membership Model

- [ ] Define target patient demographics
- [ ] Research local DPC and healthcare pricing
- [ ] Calculate required revenue to cover costs
- [ ] Determine desired panel size
- [ ] Select base model structure (flat, tiered, family, etc.)
- [ ] Define what is included in membership
- [ ] Define what costs extra (and pricing)
- [ ] Create pricing for all scenarios (individual, couple, family)
- [ ] Decide on annual vs. monthly payment options
- [ ] Draft membership agreement reflecting model
- [ ] Test pricing with potential patients (informal feedback)
- [ ] Finalize and document pricing structure
